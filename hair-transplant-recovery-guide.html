Recovery Timeline: Day by Day, Month by Month

Every patient heals at their own pace, but this timeline reflects what most people experience after the combined FUE+DHI procedure performed by Dr. Erik Yáñez at IHTC. Understanding each phase helps you stay calm during the normal ups and downs of hair restoration.

Day 1–3
Immediate Post-Op

Swelling, Soreness & Rest

The day of your procedure, the scalp is numb from local anaesthetic. As this wears off, expect mild to moderate tenderness in both the donor area (back and sides of the scalp) and the recipient zone (top and front). Swelling typically peaks on Day 2–3 and may migrate to the forehead and upper eyelids — this is normal and temporary. Sleep with your head elevated at 45° to reduce swelling and protect new grafts. Avoid touching or scratching the recipient zone. IHTC provides a post-op care kit with a saline spray, antibiotics, and anti-inflammatory medication to keep you comfortable.

Week 1
Gentle Care Phase

First Wash, Redness Fading

From Day 3 or 4 onward you'll begin gentle washing with the diluted shampoo technique your IHTC nurse demonstrates. Tepid water, no direct pressure — pour water over the scalp and let it run. The recipient area will look pink or red, with small dried blood spots or crusts around each implanted follicle. This is expected. The grafts are anchoring during this critical first week — they are fragile, so avoid hats that press down, direct sun, and anything that might snag the scalp. By Day 7 the redness has usually lightened noticeably and the grafts are physically secure.

Week 2–3
Visible Healing

Crusting Resolves, Return to Work

The small scabs or crusts soften and fall away naturally during regular washing — do not pick them. Redness continues to fade and becomes easy to conceal with light styling by Day 10–14. Most patients with desk or office jobs return to work between Day 7 and Day 14. The donor area is usually fully comfortable by Week 2, with only faint pink marks that will fade over the following weeks. You may still notice some mild itching as the skin heals — this is normal. Avoid swimming, saunas, and strenuous exercise for at least another two weeks.

Week 4–6
⚠ NORMAL — Don't Panic

The Shedding Phase — This Is Expected

The transplanted hair shafts will begin to fall out. This is called the shedding or telogen phase, and it happens to almost every patient. The follicles are responding to the mild trauma of transplantation by entering a temporary resting state. The hair falls — the follicle stays. Each follicle remains fully alive, anchored in the scalp, and will re-enter its active growth cycle on its own timetable. Many patients feel alarmed watching their new hair fall away, but this phase is a sign that the biological recovery process is working normally. After shedding, the scalp may look similar to before the procedure — this is temporary. Permanent growth begins in the weeks and months that follow.

Month 2–3

Dormant Phase — Patience Required

After shedding, many patients enter a quiet period where very little visible change happens. The follicles are in their resting telogen phase underground, preparing for the active growth (anagen) phase. This is one of the hardest parts psychologically — the scalp looks similar to before surgery and results feel far away. Trust the process. The biological clock is ticking beneath the surface. Keep following your post-op washing routine, apply any prescribed serums, and keep your follow-up appointments with the IHTC team so they can confirm progress.

Month 4–5
First Regrowth

Fine Hair Emerges — The Moment You've Waited For

Follicles begin re-entering the anagen (active growth) phase and push new hair shafts through the scalp. The initial hairs are fine, wispy, and may feel slightly different in texture — this is normal as new hair adapts to its new environment. You'll start to see a subtle improvement in the treated areas. Not all follicles re-enter growth at the same time, so coverage is still uneven at this stage. Growth rate averages around 1–1.5 cm per month. Excitement is typically building — you can see that it's working.

Month 6–8
Significant Coverage

Visible Density — Results Becoming Clear

By month 6, most patients have visible, meaningful hair coverage in the treated zone. The transplanted hairs are longer and thicker than the initial fine wisps, and the hairline is clearly taking shape. Around 60–70% of transplanted follicles are typically in active growth by this point, though the individual ratio varies. Photos taken at month 6 versus month 1 show a striking transformation. Hair texture begins normalising — the initial coarseness softens and starts to match your native hair more closely.

Month 10–12
Near-Final Results

Strong Coverage, Hairline Defined

At one year, around 80–90% of the final result is visible. The hairline is well-defined, density is strong across the treated area, and the hair moves and behaves naturally. Most patients are thrilled with results at this stage. Hair thickness and calibre continue to mature slightly as each follicle completes its first full growth cycle in its new location. IHTC recommends a 12-month review photo set to compare with your baseline images from the day of surgery.

Month 12–18
Full Mature Results

Final Density — Complete Transformation

The remaining follicles reach full maturity. Hair thickness, calibre, and natural growth patterns are now fully established. This is your long-term, permanent result. The transplanted follicles are genetically resistant to the hormone DHT that causes pattern baldness, so they will continue to grow for life. At 18 months you have the complete picture of your transformation — and the foundation for decades of natural hair going forward.

What Makes DHI Recovery Different

Not all hair transplant techniques heal the same way. The Choi Implanter Pen used in the DHI component of the combined FUE+DHI procedure creates a meaningful difference in how the recipient scalp heals.

The Choi Implanter Pen Advantage

Traditional slit-and-place hair transplant methods require two separate steps at the recipient site: first a channel is cut into the scalp with a blade, then the graft is placed into that open slit. This means each implantation site sustains two separate moments of trauma, and many slits remain open simultaneously, increasing surface disruption.

The Choi Implanter Pen used in DHI combines both steps into one precise motion. The hollow needle tip punctures the scalp and deposits the graft in a single controlled movement. The channel is no larger than the graft itself, tissue displacement is minimal, and each site closes almost immediately around the follicle. The result is less overall surface trauma, reduced bleeding during the procedure, and — for most patients — a noticeably more comfortable recovery in the first week compared to slit-based methods.

This also means a lower risk of follicle dislodgement during the early anchoring days, tighter control over angle and direction for a natural-looking final result, and faster resolution of redness because fewer surrounding cells are disturbed in the process.

Post-Op Instructions at a Glance

Dr. Erik Yáñez and the IHTC nursing team will walk you through every detail at your post-op briefing. Here is a quick-reference summary of the key guidelines for the first weeks of recovery.

Sleep Position

Sleep with your head elevated at 45° for the first 5–7 days. Use the travel pillow provided in your care kit. Avoid sleeping on the recipient area.

Washing

Begin gentle saline sprays on Day 1. Start diluted shampoo washing from Day 3–4 onward, using the pour-over method — no rubbing or direct jet pressure on the recipient zone.

Sun Exposure

Avoid direct sun on the scalp for 4 weeks. UV radiation can cause pigmentation changes in healing skin and increase swelling. Wear a loose, soft hat when outdoors after Week 1.

Exercise

Light walking from Day 4. No gym, running, cycling, or sweating until Week 2. No heavy lifting, contact sports, or swimming until Week 4.

Alcohol

Avoid alcohol for at least 5–7 days post-procedure. Alcohol dilates blood vessels, raises blood pressure, and can increase swelling and bleeding risk around new grafts.

Medications

Take the antibiotic course and anti-inflammatory as prescribed. Avoid blood thinners (aspirin, ibuprofen) unless medically necessary. Inform the team of any regular medications.

Signs Everything Is Going Well vs When to Call the Clinic

Recovery has predictable milestones. Here is how to tell the difference between what is normal and what warrants a call to the IHTC team.

 Normal & Expected

  • Mild to moderate tenderness in donor and recipient areas, Days 1–5
  • Swelling of forehead or upper eyelids, Days 2–4
  • Pinkness or redness over the recipient zone, first 1–2 weeks
  • Small dry scabs or crusts around each graft, first 7–14 days
  • Mild itching as the skin heals, Week 2–4
  • Transplanted hair shedding, Weeks 4–6
  • Dormant-looking scalp, Months 2–3
  • Fine, thin regrowth emerging unevenly, Months 4–5
  • Slight numbness in the donor or recipient area, first 4–8 weeks

 Contact IHTC If You Notice

  • Sudden sharp pain that worsens rather than improves after Day 5
  • Significant bleeding from the recipient zone after Day 3
  • Signs of infection: increasing redness, warmth, yellow discharge, or fever
  • A graft or cluster of grafts appears to have been dislodged or pulled out
  • Swelling that is not improving by Day 5 or is spreading significantly
  • Allergic reaction symptoms: hives, difficulty breathing, severe rash
  • Complete absence of any regrowth by Month 6
  • Any concern — the IHTC team would always rather you reach out early

Recovery FAQ

The five questions patients ask most often about hair transplant recovery, answered by the IHTC clinical team.

How long does hair transplant recovery take?

Full recovery — meaning completely mature, final results — takes 12–18 months. However, visible wound healing is mostly done within 2–3 weeks, and most patients return to normal daily life, including work, within 1–2 weeks of the combined FUE+DHI procedure. The lengthy timeline is about hair growth, not wounds: follicles have their own biological clock, and the full result simply takes time.

Is the shedding phase normal after a hair transplant?

Yes — completely. The shedding phase between weeks 4 and 6 is one of the most predictable parts of recovery and does not mean anything went wrong. Transplanted hair shafts fall as the follicles enter a temporary resting phase in response to the mild trauma of transplantation. The follicles themselves stay anchored and alive in the scalp and will produce new, permanent hair as they re-enter the active growth phase. Think of shedding as the "restart" that precedes long-term growth.

When can I go back to work after a hair transplant?

Most patients with desk or office jobs return to work between Days 7 and 14. By the end of Week 2, redness has significantly faded and remaining crusting is easy to conceal with light styling. If your work involves heavy physical labour, prolonged sun exposure, or a dusty or dirty environment, waiting until Week 4–6 is advisable. Your IHTC team will provide a personalised return-to-work recommendation during your post-op follow-up based on your specific healing progress.

When can I exercise after a hair transplant?

Light walking is safe from Day 4 onward. Avoid any activity that raises your heart rate significantly or causes sweating for the first 10–14 days — increased blood pressure can affect graft stability in the early anchoring phase. Easy cardio such as brisk walking or gentle cycling can resume around Week 2. Gym training, weightlifting, and any contact sports should wait until Week 4. Swimming pools, hot tubs, and open water should be avoided for at least 4 weeks to prevent infection.

How much does recovery hurt after a hair transplant?

Most patients describe post-operative discomfort as mild to moderate — more like a sunburn than sharp pain. The donor area is typically the more tender of the two zones for the first 3–5 days. The recipient area is often less uncomfortable because the Choi Implanter Pen used in the DHI component of the procedure creates a very precise, minimal channel rather than longer incisions. The take-home pain management kit IHTC provides makes the first few days comfortable for the vast majority of patients, and serious pain is uncommon. If discomfort is not manageable with the provided medication, contact the clinic immediately.
