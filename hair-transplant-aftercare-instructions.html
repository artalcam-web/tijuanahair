Why Aftercare Matters

After your combined FUE+DHI procedure, the transplanted follicles are physically present in their new locations — but they are not yet anchored with a blood supply. The first 7–14 days are the critical window in which each follicle establishes its connection to the surrounding tissue.

During this time, follicles are delicate. Actions that increase blood pressure to the scalp, introduce bacteria, expose the area to direct sun, or mechanically disrupt the grafts — rubbing, scratching, tight hats, bending over — can reduce graft survival. The good news: following aftercare instructions is one of the most controllable factors in determining your final result. What you do in those first two weeks makes a measurable difference.

  Key principle: Think of your grafts like newly planted seeds in a garden. They need a calm environment, gentle water, and protection from harsh conditions until they take root. Once rooted — typically after 10–14 days — they become permanent and much more robust.

Recovery Timeline: Phase by Phase

The timeline below reflects what most patients experience after the combined FUE+DHI procedure at IHTC. Your IHTC team will give you a personalised schedule with exact dates. Always follow their version.

🌙
First 24 Hours
Immediate Post-Op

Rest, Elevation & Medication

Sleep with your head elevated at approximately 45 degrees — extra pillows or a wedge pillow work well. Elevation prevents swelling from pooling in the forehead and recipient area, and reduces pressure on the new grafts. Keep the recipient area completely dry. Take prescribed medications as directed by Dr. Yáñez (typically an antibiotic and an anti-inflammatory). Avoid bending over or lifting heavy objects, as these activities raise blood pressure in the scalp. Do not touch, rub, or scratch the recipient area under any circumstances. Mild swelling and a sensation of tightness around the recipient zone are normal and expected.

🧴
Days 2–7
Scabbing Phase

Gentle Washing Begins — Scabs Form Naturally

Gentle washing typically starts on Day 2 or Day 3, following the specific method Dr. Yáñez provides. Use the saline spray or washing solution given to you at the clinic. When rinsing, pour water gently over the scalp — never apply direct pressure from a showerhead. Pat dry softly with a clean towel; never rub. Small scabs will form around each implanted follicle — this is a normal part of healing and they will fall away naturally by approximately Day 10–14. Do not pick or scratch scabs. The donor area (small red dots from the extraction sites) typically heals within 7–10 days. Mild itching may occur — resist the urge to scratch.

🏃
Weeks 2–4
Gradual Return

Desk Work Returns — Activity Still Limited

Most patients with desk-based jobs can return to work around Day 5–7, once redness is fading and any remaining scabs are manageable. Continue to avoid gyms, heavy exercise, and activities that cause heavy sweating for at least 2 full weeks — elevated heart rate means elevated scalp blood pressure, which can disrupt graft anchoring. Swimming pools, hot tubs, and saunas should be avoided due to infection risk. When going outdoors, protect the recipient area from direct sunlight by wearing a loose, wide-brimmed hat — avoid anything with a tight band or strap across the recipient zone.

⚠️
Weeks 4–6
⚠ NORMAL — Don't Panic

The Shedding Phase — This Is Expected

Transplanted hairs will shed during this period. This is completely normal and happens to nearly every patient. The hair shafts fall out as the follicles enter a temporary resting phase in response to the mild trauma of transplantation. The follicle is alive — only the hair shaft falls. Each follicle remains firmly anchored in the scalp and will re-enter the active growth phase to produce new, permanent hair. Many patients panic when they see hair in the shower or on their pillow during this phase — do not. If you are concerned or uncertain whether what you are seeing is normal, contact IHTC via WhatsApp. That is what the team is there for.

😴
Month 2–3
Dormant Phase

Little Visible Change — Follicles Are Resting

This is often the most psychologically challenging phase: the shedding is complete, but new growth has not yet appeared. The follicles are resting (in telogen phase) and silently preparing for active growth. This is normal. Continue to avoid any trauma to the scalp and follow any ongoing care instructions from IHTC. The first fine hairs typically begin to emerge around months 4–5. By months 6–8, meaningful coverage becomes visible. Near-final density is usually reached by months 10–12, with full maturity at 12–18 months.

Hair Transplant Do's & Don'ts

A quick reference for the first 2–4 weeks after your procedure — the period when your choices have the greatest impact on graft survival and healing.

DO

  • Sleep with head elevated at 45° for the first 5–7 nights
  • Follow Dr. Yáñez's specific washing instructions precisely
  • Pat the scalp dry gently with a clean, soft towel
  • Take all prescribed medications for the full course
  • Use the saline spray as directed during the first few days
  • Wear a loose hat outdoors to protect from sun (not tight-fitting)
  • Contact IHTC via WhatsApp if you have any questions
  • Allow scabs to fall off naturally — they will, by Day 10–14

DON'T

  • Touch, rub, or scratch the recipient or donor area
  • Pick at scabs — this can dislodge grafts and cause scarring
  • Bend over or lift heavy objects in the first 48 hours
  • Exercise heavily or visit the gym for at least 2 weeks
  • Swim in pools, hot tubs, or the ocean for at least 4 weeks
  • Expose the recipient area to direct, intense sunlight
  • Use any hair products not approved by IHTC in the first week
  • Apply harsh chemical treatments in the first 6 months

Longer-Term Restrictions

Beyond the first few weeks, most normal activities can gradually return. However, a small number of restrictions apply over a longer timeframe to protect the longevity of your result.

Scalp Tattooing Over Transplant Area

Direct tattooing over a transplanted area carries permanent risk of follicle damage and should be avoided indefinitely.

Harsh Chemical Treatments (First 6 Months)

Chemical relaxers, perms, bleach, and similar treatments should not be applied to the transplanted area for at least 6 months.

Significant Scalp Trauma

Any procedure or activity that involves meaningful trauma to the scalp — friction, abrasion, or pressure — should be discussed with IHTC before proceeding.

Sun Exposure (Ongoing)

Once healed, general sun protection for the scalp is good practice to preserve graft health and prevent hyperpigmentation at extraction sites.

Signs to Contact IHTC Immediately

Most post-procedure symptoms — mild swelling, tenderness, scabbing, itching, and shedding — are normal. However, some signs warrant immediate contact with the clinic. When in doubt, always reach out.

Contact IHTC Right Away If You Notice:

  • Signs of infection: increasing redness spreading beyond the wound site, unusual warmth, discharge or pus, or a fever
  • Unusual or painful swelling that develops or worsens after Day 3 (mild swelling in the first 48–72 hours is normal)
  • Grafts appearing to have come out as intact units — there is an important difference between normal hair shedding (the shaft falls, the follicle stays) and a graft being physically displaced
  • Significant or persistent bleeding from either the recipient or donor area
  • Any concern you are unsure about — the IHTC team is available via WhatsApp and would rather hear from you than have you worry alone

Frequently Asked Questions: Post-Op Care

The most common questions patients ask about recovery after the combined FUE+DHI procedure at IHTC.

When can I wash my hair after a hair transplant?

Most patients begin gentle washing on Day 2 or Day 3 after the procedure. Dr. Yáñez and the IHTC team will provide you with the specific washing protocol — including which solution to use and the exact technique — during your post-op briefing. The key rule in the first week: pour water gently over the scalp and pat dry with a soft towel. Never rub. Normal shampooing resumes once all scabs have naturally fallen off, usually around Day 10–14.

Can I sleep normally after a hair transplant?

For the first 5–7 nights, sleep with your head elevated at approximately 45 degrees — typically achieved with extra pillows or a wedge pillow. Elevation reduces swelling by preventing fluid from pooling in the forehead and recipient area, and also protects new grafts from being accidentally rubbed against a flat pillow. After the first week, once scabs have firmed and grafts are more securely anchored, you can gradually return to your normal sleeping position.

When can I exercise after a hair transplant?

Light walking is generally fine from around Day 4 onward. Any exercise that significantly raises your heart rate or causes heavy sweating should be avoided for at least 2 weeks. Light cardio (brisk walking, easy cycling) can typically resume around Week 2. Gym training, weightlifting, and contact sports should wait until Week 4. Swimming should be avoided for at least 4 weeks to prevent infection risk. Always follow the specific guidance from your IHTC team, which takes precedence.

What is the shedding phase and is it normal?

The shedding phase occurs between approximately weeks 4 and 6 after a hair transplant. The transplanted hair shafts fall out as the follicles enter a temporary resting (telogen) phase. This is completely normal and expected — it happens to nearly every patient. Crucially, the follicle itself remains alive and rooted in the scalp. It will re-enter the active growth phase and produce new, permanent hair. The shedding does not mean the procedure failed. Contact IHTC via WhatsApp if you are concerned.

When should I call the clinic after my procedure?

Contact IHTC immediately if you notice signs of infection (increasing redness, warmth, discharge, or fever), unusual or painful swelling after Day 3, any area where grafts appear to have come out as intact units (versus normal hair shedding), or significant or persistent bleeding. For any non-urgent question or concern during recovery, the IHTC team is available via WhatsApp and is happy to review photos of your scalp and advise you directly.
