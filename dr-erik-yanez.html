Hairline Design Is a Science. Dr. Yáñez Treats It Like One.

Most clinics draw a line and call it a design. Dr. Yáñez measures before he draws anything.

A transplanted hairline that looks artificial almost always fails for the same reason: it was placed where the surgeon estimated it should go, not where the patient's facial proportions say it should be. Dr. Yáñez's methodology starts from a different premise — that every face has its own geometry, and the hairline must follow that geometry precisely to look natural.

Over 5 years of dedicated practice in hair restoration and facial aesthetics, Dr. Yáñez has developed a measurement protocol he applies to every patient before any graft is discussed.

01

Facial Thirds

The face divides naturally into three equal horizontal thirds: hairline to brow, brow to nose base, nose base to chin. Dr. Yáñez uses this ratio — not a fixed centimeter measurement — to determine where the hairline should begin for each individual patient. A line placed even a few millimeters outside this proportion reads as unnatural to the human eye.

02

Inter-Eye Distance

The distance between the inner corners of the eyes defines the natural width of the central hairline zone. Dr. Yáñez uses this measurement to ensure the hairline doesn't start too wide (creating a boxy, transplanted look) or too narrow (creating an unnatural widow's peak). This single measurement is often what separates a convincing result from an obvious one.

03

Temporal Recession Angle

The angle at which the hairline recedes toward the temples must be appropriate for the patient's age, face shape, and bone structure. Dr. Yáñez calculates this angle individually — too aggressive an angle looks artificial; too straight a line looks juvenile. The right angle makes the hairline read as naturally evolved, not reconstructed.

04

Midline Symmetry

Every face has a structural midline. The hairline is calibrated to this vertical axis so that both sides are balanced — not mirror-identical (which also looks artificial), but harmoniously symmetrical, the way natural hairlines actually are. Dr. Yáñez marks this reference point on every patient before design begins.

05

Micro-Graft Edge Technique

Even a perfectly placed hairline looks transplanted if it has a hard, uniform edge. Dr. Yáñez places single-hair micro-grafts along the very front boundary of the design — staggered irregularly, the way natural hair actually grows. The result is a soft, graduated edge that blends into the skin without any visible line of demarcation.

The result?

A hairline that looks like it was always there — not placed, not drawn, not transplanted. Patients' friends and family don't know. They just notice you look better.
