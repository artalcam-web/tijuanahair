Step-by-Step: What Dr. Yáñez Evaluates

Every IHTC consultation follows a structured seven-point clinical assessment designed to give you a precise, personalized plan.

1

Hair Loss Stage Assessment

Dr. Yáñez identifies your Norwood stage from photos and examination. The Norwood scale runs from Stage I (no significant loss) to Stage VII (severe loss with minimal hair remaining). Your stage determines the scope of the procedure, the zones that need coverage, and how many grafts are needed. If you don't know your stage yet, the consultation answers that question precisely.

2

Donor Area Evaluation

The donor zone — the permanent hair band at the back and sides of the scalp — is your source of follicles. Dr. Yáñez assesses the density, caliber, and total available grafts in this zone. This evaluation is critical: it determines what's achievable and ensures the donor area isn't over-harvested, which would create visible thinning at the back after surgery.

3

Hair Characteristics

Hair thickness, curl pattern, and color relative to scalp color all affect how many grafts you'll need and what the result will look like. Thicker, curlier hair provides more visual coverage per graft than fine, straight hair. A good contrast between hair and scalp color means individual grafts show more. Dr. Yáñez factors all of this into the graft estimate and procedure plan.

4

Recipient Zone Mapping

The recipient zone is the area or areas to be covered: hairline, temples, crown, or a combination. Dr. Yáñez maps the full recipient zone against your current loss pattern, your available donor supply, and your goals. This mapping determines how grafts are distributed across zones and informs the overall graft count.

5

Hairline Design

For procedures that include the frontal hairline, Dr. Yáñez designs the proposed hairline during the consultation. This design accounts for your facial structure, your age, and — critically — the long-term trajectory of your hair loss. A hairline designed for a 30-year-old should still look appropriate and natural at 50, even if surrounding native hair continues to thin. This is one of the most consequential decisions in the procedure, and Dr. Yáñez takes it seriously.

6

Graft Count Estimate

Based on everything above — your Norwood stage, donor availability, hair characteristics, recipient zone, and hairline design — Dr. Yáñez delivers a specific graft count recommendation. This is a clinical estimate, not a range designed to be negotiated. It reflects the actual number of grafts needed to achieve the planned coverage in the combined FUE+DHI procedure.

7

Cost Estimate

Your graft count determines your final cost, which Dr. Yáñez confirms at the consultation. The cost estimate you receive is specific to your case, based on your confirmed graft count, with no hidden fees.

Questions to Ask at Your Consultation

A good consultation is a two-way conversation. These ten questions cut through the noise and give you the information that actually matters.

1

Who performs the entire procedure — surgeon or technician? Is Dr. Yáñez hands-on throughout, or does a team take over after extraction?

2

How many years has Dr. Yáñez been performing the combined FUE+DHI procedure, and approximately how many procedures has he completed?

3

Can I see before-and-after photos of patients at my specific Norwood stage, and ideally with similar hair characteristics to mine?

4

What graft survival rate does the clinic typically achieve with the combined FUE+DHI technique, and what contributes to variation in that rate?

5

What does post-operative support look like? Is there a follow-up schedule, and who do I contact if I have concerns after I'm home?

6

What if I'm not satisfied with the results? What is the clinic's policy if the outcome doesn't match the plan established at the consultation?

7

What happens to my graft count if my hair loss continues and I need a second session later — will I still have sufficient donor supply?

8

How many procedures does the clinic perform per day? Is my procedure the only one that day, or are multiple sessions scheduled simultaneously?

9

What anesthesia is used and how is discomfort managed during the procedure? What should I expect in terms of pain on the day?

10

What is the cancellation and rescheduling policy if my plans change after booking a procedure date?

Red Flags in Any Hair Transplant Consultation

These warning signs apply to any clinic, not just IHTC. Be cautious if you encounter any of them.

No surgeon involvement in the consultation. If your consultation is handled entirely by a patient coordinator or technician with no surgeon participation, be skeptical. The person assessing your case should be medically qualified to do so — ideally the surgeon who would perform the procedure.

Guaranteed results in writing. No ethical surgeon guarantees an exact cosmetic outcome. Hair transplant results depend on many biological factors including graft survival, native hair loss progression, and individual healing. A guarantee of a specific "final look" is a red flag, not a selling point.

Pressure to book immediately. A legitimate consultation gives you time to decide. If you're urged to confirm a date during the call, warned that "pricing goes up tomorrow," or made to feel that delaying means losing your spot, the clinic's priorities are misaligned with yours.

Pricing given before examining your photos. A cost estimate that appears before anyone has evaluated your hair loss is not a medical estimate — it's a sales hook. Legitimate pricing requires a specific graft count, and a graft count requires looking at your actual case.

No questions about your hair loss history and progression. A thorough consultation includes questions about when your loss started, how fast it has progressed, and your family history. A clinic that skips this is skipping information that directly affects whether the procedure will produce lasting results.
