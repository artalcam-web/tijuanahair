Logistics Guide for US Patients

Crossing Back to the US After Your Procedure

Most IHTC patients return home to California the same evening. Here's exactly what to expect.

International Hair Transplant Clinic (IHTC) · Dr. Erik Yáñez, MD · Updated October 2026

✓ The Short Answer

Yes — you can cross the US border the same day as your hair transplant, and most IHTC patients do. IHTC's combined FUE+DHI procedure typically finishes in the early afternoon. Most patients are on their way back to San Ysidro by 3–5 PM and home in California by dinner. There are no medical restrictions on crossing the border after a hair transplant.

What Your Head Looks Like at the Border

The most common anxiety we hear from first-time patients is: "What will the border agent think when they see my head?" The honest answer: probably nothing unusual.

Here's what you'll actually look like on Day 1 of recovery:

  • Donor area (back and sides of scalp): Small red dots — the extraction sites from the FUE portion of the procedure. These look like a mild sunburn or a case of folliculitis. Not alarming or visibly surgical.
  • Recipient area (hairline and top): May have light gauze bandaging, or may be exposed with tiny implanted grafts visible at the surface. The skin may appear slightly pink or reddish.
  • Swelling: Mild forehead swelling is normal on Day 1–2, and often looks more like puffiness than anything procedure-related.
  • Overall appearance: You are not visibly injured. You will be awake, alert, and walking normally. The procedure is performed entirely under local anesthesia — no general anesthesia, no sedation (unless specifically requested), no IV drips you need to be attached to.
Border agents are accustomed to medical tourism from Tijuana. San Ysidro is the world's busiest land port of entry. CBP officers regularly see patients returning from dental procedures, orthopedic injections, cosmetic treatments, and outpatient surgeries. Your appearance on Day 1 post-procedure is unlikely to attract any special attention. You do not need to volunteer medical information. If asked about the purpose of your visit, you may simply say you had a minor outpatient procedure.

Packing the right things before your procedure day makes the return trip smooth. Here's your complete crossing checklist:

San Ysidro vs. Otay Mesa: Which Crossing to Use

There are two primary vehicle crossings between Tijuana and the San Diego area. For IHTC patients, the choice is straightforward:

★ Recommended
Primary Option

San Ysidro (I-5 / CA-117)

Closest crossing to IHTC's Zona Río location — approximately 10–20 minutes by car. Most direct route back to San Diego, Los Angeles, and all of Southern California. This is the standard crossing for IHTC patients.

Alternative Option

Otay Mesa (SR-905)

Located about 10 minutes east of San Ysidro. A solid backup when San Ysidro wait times spike. Often shorter waits on weekdays. Connects to SR-905 and I-905, which merges with I-805 north toward San Diego.

SENTRI / Trusted Traveler

SENTRI Lane (San Ysidro or Otay Mesa)

If you have a SENTRI card, use the dedicated express lane — wait times can be dramatically shorter, often under 10 minutes even during busy periods. Well worth it for frequent border crossers.

Airport Passengers Only

Cross Border Xpress (CBX)

The pedestrian bridge connecting San Diego directly to Tijuana International Airport (TIJ). Relevant only if you are flying in or out of TIJ. IHTC patients driving from San Diego do not need CBX.

Border Wait Times: What to Expect

Wait times at San Ysidro are the most variable factor in your return trip. They can range from under 20 minutes to over two hours depending on the day and hour. Here's how to plan:

How to Check Live Wait Times

  • CBP Border Wait Times app — the official US Customs and Border Protection app shows real-time waits by lane (standard, SENTRI, Ready Lane) at both San Ysidro and Otay Mesa. Free on iOS and Android.
  • Google Maps — search "San Ysidro border crossing" and you'll see live traffic conditions and typical delay estimates. Highly accurate for vehicle lanes.
  • bwtinfo.com — dedicated border wait time tracking for the California-Baja California crossings.

Your Step-by-Step Return Crossing

Here's exactly what the return trip looks like from the moment your procedure ends at IHTC to arriving home in California:

1
Finish your procedure and receive post-op instructions

Dr. Yáñez reviews your recovery protocol before you leave the clinic. You'll receive written aftercare instructions, your prescribed medications, and the IHTC WhatsApp number. This typically takes 15–20 minutes after the procedure itself concludes.

2
Check live wait times before departing

Before leaving the clinic, open the CBP Border Wait Times app or Google Maps and check current conditions at San Ysidro. If wait times are over 90 minutes, consider checking Otay Mesa as an alternative or waiting 30–45 minutes at the clinic's reception area before heading out.

3
Take IHTC's ground transport or a rideshare to the crossing

IHTC coordinates ground transportation from the clinic to San Ysidro for patients who need it — just ask the front desk when booking. Alternatively, Uber and InDriver are widely available in Zona Río. The clinic-to-border trip is approximately 10–20 minutes by car.

4
Present your documents at the CBP booth

Hand your US passport or SENTRI card to the CBP officer. If asked about the purpose of your visit, you may say "minor outpatient medical procedure." Your procedure paperwork is available to show if asked, but it is almost never requested for routine medical tourism. SENTRI card holders use the dedicated express lane.

5
Rideshare or drive home — keep your head elevated

Once across, pick up a Lyft or Uber in the San Ysidro area or meet whoever is driving you. Sit upright — do not slump forward or sleep with your head in an awkward position during the drive. Keeping your head above heart level helps minimize swelling. Most IHTC patients are home in San Diego within 30–45 minutes of crossing; Los Angeles-area patients are home in 2–3 hours.

What NOT to Do During the Crossing

Most of the return trip is straightforward, but a few specific mistakes can harm your graft survival or create unnecessary complications. Avoid these:

🚫 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ✗
    Wear a tight-fitting hat that presses on the grafts. In the first 10–14 days, newly implanted grafts are not firmly anchored. A baseball cap or any hat with a firm band that presses on the recipient area can dislodge grafts. Use a loose, wide-brim hat or a soft bucket-style hat if you need sun protection. Or go without — the scalp exposure at the crossing is brief.
  • ✗
    Sleep slumped over in the car. Sleeping upright is fine. Sleeping slumped forward or with your head pressed sideways against the car window is not — it puts direct pressure on grafts and increases facial swelling. If you're a passenger on a long drive home, recline slightly and use a neck pillow if needed, not a side pillow.
  • ✗
    Drive yourself if you received any sedation. If your procedure included any sedation (IV or oral) beyond local anesthesia, you should not drive. Arrange for someone to drive you. Most IHTC patients receive only local anesthesia and are fully alert by the time the procedure ends, but confirm with Dr. Yáñez on the day of your procedure.
  • ✗
    Leave your medications at the clinic. Your prescribed post-op medications are part of your recovery protocol. They'll be given to you before you leave IHTC, but make sure you have them in your bag before heading to the border. Losing or forgetting them means sourcing them at a US pharmacy, which adds time and cost.
  • ✗
    Be in direct sun without protection for extended periods. During the crossing — particularly if you're waiting in a vehicle queue — prolonged direct sun exposure on the fresh recipient area should be minimized. The loose hat helps. For pedestrian crossers, the walk itself is brief.
  • ✗
    Touch or scratch the scalp during the drive. Itching is common in the first few days as healing begins. Scratching or rubbing the recipient area — including absentmindedly touching it in the car — can dislodge grafts. Keep your hands away from your scalp.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I cross the US border the same day as my hair transplant?
Yes. There are no medical restrictions on crossing the US border after a hair transplant procedure. IHTC's combined FUE+DHI procedure typically finishes in the early afternoon, and most patients are crossing at San Ysidro by 3–5 PM and home in California by dinner. You'll be awake, alert, and fully mobile — the procedure is performed under local anesthesia.
What do I tell border agents about my hair transplant?
You are not required to volunteer any medical information at the border. CBP officers at San Ysidro see medical tourism patients constantly. If asked about the purpose of your visit, simply say you had a minor outpatient procedure. Your scalp on Day 1 — small red extraction dots, possible light bandaging — does not look alarming or surgical to a CBP officer. IHTC provides procedure paperwork you can show if asked, though this is rarely requested.
How long is the wait at San Ysidro after a hair transplant?
Wait times vary by day and hour. Tuesday through Thursday afternoons are typically the fastest — often 20–45 minutes. Friday evenings and weekends can reach 90–150 minutes. Check the CBP Border Wait Times app or Google Maps before you leave the clinic. If San Ysidro is backed up, Otay Mesa (about 10 minutes east) is usually a viable alternative with shorter waits.
Do I need any special documents to cross after a hair transplant?
No special documents are required. You need the same identification you used to enter Mexico: a US passport, passport card, SENTRI card, or Trusted Traveler document. IHTC provides procedure paperwork — a summary of your treatment — which you may carry but are not required to present. US citizens and permanent residents re-enter normally.
Can I fly home the same day as my hair transplant?
Flying on the same day as your procedure is generally not recommended. Cabin pressure changes, dry recirculated air, the physical demands of navigating an airport, and the temptation to sleep slumped in a seat all create unnecessary risk for fresh grafts. Most IHTC patients drive or rideshare back across San Ysidro the same day and, if they're flying home from San Diego or Los Angeles, take their flight after 48–72 hours. If you need to fly sooner, discuss it with Dr. Yáñez — certain shorter domestic flights may be acceptable with precautions.
