FUE vs DHI: Understanding Both Techniques

At IHTC, these aren't competing options — they work together in every procedure. Here's why.

The Question Patients Ask

When researching hair transplants, most patients encounter the question "FUE or DHI?" as if it's a binary choice. Clinic websites, online forums, and YouTube videos frame the two as competing techniques — each with advantages and disadvantages — and ask patients to pick a side.

This framing is misleading. FUE and DHI do not describe two different hair transplants. They describe two different phases of the same procedure: FUE describes how grafts are extracted from the donor area, while DHI describes how grafts are implanted into the recipient area. A complete hair transplant always involves both extraction and implantation — the only question is which methods are used for each.

This page explains exactly what each technique does, how they differ, why they are complementary rather than competing, and why IHTC combines both in every procedure rather than asking patients to choose between them.

What Is FUE?

Follicular Unit Extraction — the gold-standard extraction method used to harvest individual hair follicles from the donor area.

Extraction Phase

FUE stands for Follicular Unit Extraction. It is the technique used during the extraction phase of a hair transplant — the phase in which individual hair follicles are harvested from the donor area (typically the back and sides of the scalp) before being implanted.

A micro-punch tool — typically 0.7mm to 1.0mm in diameter — is used to score around each follicular unit and extract it cleanly, one graft at a time. Each follicular unit naturally contains one to four hairs. The result is thousands of individual grafts that can be implanted in the thinning or balding area.

Because FUE uses circular punches rather than a strip incision, it leaves no linear scar. Tiny circular punch marks heal within 7–10 days and become virtually invisible once the donor area's surrounding hair grows back. This makes short haircut styles fully compatible with recovery.

FUE replaced the older FUT (Follicular Unit Transplantation, also called "strip harvesting") method as the dominant extraction technique. Unlike FUT, which surgically removes a strip of scalp and leaves a permanent linear scar, FUE preserves the donor area cosmetically.

No linear scar — only tiny circular punch marks
Heals invisibly within 7–10 days
Allows short haircut styles post-recovery
Suitable for most hair loss patterns and hair types

FUE is an extraction technique — implantation is a separate step

What Is DHI?

Direct Hair Implantation — the precision implantation method using the Choi Implanter Pen to place each graft with exact control.

Implantation Phase

DHI stands for Direct Hair Implantation. It is also known as the Choi Implanter Pen technique, after the hollow needle instrument used to perform it. DHI is the technique used during the implantation phase of a hair transplant — the phase in which extracted grafts are placed into the recipient area.

In traditional implantation (often called slit-and-place), a surgeon first creates all the recipient channels (small slits or holes) across the scalp, and then a second technician inserts the grafts into those pre-made channels. The two steps are sequential and separate.

In DHI, the Choi Implanter Pen loads a single graft into its hollow needle tip and implants it in one motion — creating the recipient channel and placing the graft simultaneously. This gives the surgeon direct control over the angle, depth, and direction of every implanted hair in real time, without pre-made channels.

The result is more natural growth direction (especially important at the hairline and crown), higher achievable density because grafts can be placed closer together, and less trauma to the recipient scalp because channels are not pre-opened and left empty.

Precise control over graft angle, depth, and direction
No pre-made channels — scalp tissue holds graft during implant
Higher natural density achievable
Reduced trauma to recipient scalp
Excellent results at hairline (forward-growing grafts)
Excellent results at crown (complex whorl pattern)
DHI is an implantation technique — extraction is a separate step

The Critical Difference: Extraction vs Implantation

FUE and DHI are not competing full techniques — they address different phases of the procedure. This is the point most online comparisons miss.

The Core Misunderstanding

FUE describes HOW grafts are extracted. It says nothing about how they are implanted.

DHI describes HOW grafts are implanted. It says nothing about how they were extracted.

A clinic that advertises "FUE only" is telling you about their extraction method — but they still have to implant the grafts somehow, often using the older slit-and-place method. A clinic that advertises "DHI only" is telling you about their implantation method — but they still have to extract the grafts somehow, almost always using FUE.

The two techniques operate on completely different phases of the same procedure. Comparing them as if you must choose one is like asking whether a flight should use its left engine or its landing gear — they do entirely different jobs.

Extraction — FUE

FUE Handles Harvest

The micro-punch removes each follicular unit from the donor area individually. This phase determines scar characteristics, donor area appearance, and how many grafts can be harvested safely.

FUE leaves no linear scar — only circular punch marks that heal invisibly within days.

Implantation — DHI

DHI Handles Placement

The Choi Implanter Pen places each graft into the recipient area in one motion. This phase determines the angle, density, direction, and naturalness of the final result.

DHI's precision placement is particularly critical at the hairline and crown, where growth direction is most visible.

Questions to Ask Any Clinic

Before choosing a hair transplant clinic, these three questions will tell you exactly what their procedure involves — and reveal whether they are using the best method for each phase.

1
"Do you use FUE or FUT for extraction?"
FUE = no linear scar, circular punch marks that heal invisibly. FUT (strip) = permanent linear scar at the donor site. Most modern clinics use FUE. If the clinic uses FUT, understand that a permanent scar is part of the trade-off.
2
"Do you use the Choi Implanter Pen (DHI) for implantation, or slit-and-place?"
DHI = one-motion graft placement, precise angle control, less recipient trauma. Slit-and-place = channels pre-made first, then grafts inserted separately. DHI gives the surgeon more control over placement direction and density.
3
"What percentage of your procedures combine FUE extraction with DHI implantation?"
At IHTC, the answer is 100% — every procedure combines both. A clinic that uses FUE for extraction but slit-and-place for implantation is offering half the picture. A clinic that says it offers "DHI only" without clarifying the extraction method should be asked how grafts are harvested.

What is the difference between FUE and DHI?
FUE (Follicular Unit Extraction) refers to how grafts are extracted from the donor area — using micro-punches, one follicle at a time. DHI (Direct Hair Implantation) refers to how grafts are implanted using the Choi Implanter Pen. They address different phases of the same procedure and are not competing options. A complete hair transplant requires both an extraction method and an implantation method.

Neither is "better" in isolation — because they don't do the same thing. FUE describes extraction; DHI describes implantation.
