What Is a Graft?

Understanding the basic unit of hair transplant surgery is the first step toward understanding your procedure.

A graft is a single follicular unit — the naturally occurring bundle of hairs that grow together from a shared root structure beneath the scalp. Each follicular unit contains between one and four hairs, although the average is roughly two to three hairs per graft depending on the individual and the area of the scalp.

Because each graft is a complete biological unit rather than an individual strand, surgeons think and plan in grafts — not in individual hairs. When your surgeon says "you need 2,000 grafts," that typically means somewhere between 4,000 and 6,000 individual hairs will be transplanted, assuming an average of two to three hairs per graft.

At IHTC, Dr. Erik Yáñez uses the combined FUE+DHI procedure for every hair transplant. The FUE (Follicular Unit Extraction) phase harvests individual grafts one by one from the donor area at the back and sides of the scalp — leaving no linear scar and preserving the natural appearance of the donor zone. The DHI (Direct Hair Implantation) phase then places each graft precisely into the recipient area using a specialized implanter pen, controlling depth, angle, and direction with greater accuracy than traditional methods.

The result is a natural-looking hairline and density, achieved without removing a strip of scalp and without the recovery time associated with older techniques.

Factors That Determine Your Graft Count

No two patients need the same number of grafts. These are the primary variables Dr. Yáñez evaluates at every IHTC consultation.

Recipient Zone Area

The surface area of the region to be covered — measured in cm² — is the most direct driver of graft count. A larger thinning area requires more grafts to fill, all else being equal. Frontal hairline restorations cover less area than full top-of-scalp treatments, which is why graft counts vary so widely across patients.

Desired Density

Density is measured in grafts per cm². Natural, untouched scalp has roughly 60–80 follicular units per cm², but transplanted density is typically set at 35–50 grafts per cm² to ensure each graft survives and the donor area is not over-depleted. Higher target density = more grafts needed for the same area.

Hair Characteristics

Thick, coarse, or curly hair provides more visual coverage per graft than fine, straight hair — because each strand occupies more apparent space on the scalp. Patients with dark hair against a light scalp also need more grafts to achieve the same apparent density compared to patients whose hair color closely matches their scalp tone.

Donor Area Density & Availability

The number of grafts Dr. Yáñez can safely extract is limited by your donor zone — the permanent hair at the back and sides of the scalp. A dense, healthy donor area gives more flexibility; a donor area that has already thinned (or been used in a previous procedure) may cap how many grafts are available. Safe extraction never exceeds roughly 25–30% of the donor zone to preserve its long-term appearance.

The Consultation: How Dr. Yáñez Calculates Your Count

The free consultation at IHTC is a structured clinical process — not a sales call. Here's exactly what happens, step by step.

Scalp Photo Analysis

Before the consultation, you submit photos of your hairline, crown, and temples in good lighting. Dr. Yáñez reviews these in advance to form a preliminary Norwood stage assessment and identify the key areas of concern. This ensures that the consultation itself is focused on your actual situation — not spent gathering basic information.

Hairline Design

Dr. Yáñez designs your target hairline based on your facial structure, age, natural hair direction, and your stated goals. The design determines the boundaries of the recipient zone — which directly feeds into the graft count calculation. A natural hairline design is not a straight horizontal line; it follows your facial geometry and has intentional irregularity that makes it look real.

Recipient Zone Measurement

The designed hairline and thinning areas are measured to determine the total recipient zone surface area in cm². This number, multiplied by the target density (grafts per cm²), gives the baseline graft estimate. Hair characteristics — thickness, curl, color — are factored in to adjust the target density up or down.

Donor Area Density Count

Dr. Yáñez evaluates the density and caliber of your donor zone — the permanent hair at the back and sides of your scalp — and calculates how many grafts can be safely extracted without thinning or depleting the area. This establishes the upper limit of what is achievable. If donor supply falls short of the calculated need, Dr. Yáñez discusses prioritization: which areas to treat first for the most visual impact.

Final Graft Recommendation

With recipient zone measurements and donor supply data in hand, Dr. Yáñez delivers a final graft count recommendation — the number that achieves your goals within the safe limits of your donor area. You receive a projected cost based on the current promotional price from $2,345 USD. There is no commitment required at this stage; the consultation is free, and the recommendation comes with no obligation to book.

Can I Get More Grafts Later?

Second-session planning is a key part of how Dr. Yáñez approaches every case — because hair loss doesn't always stop after your first procedure.

Second Sessions Are Possible

Yes — patients can return for a second procedure if they want more coverage, if their hair loss has progressed since the first transplant, or if the first session prioritized one area (such as the frontal hairline) and the crown was left for later. The timing and feasibility depend on how much donor density was preserved in the first session.

Donor Area Is Planned for the Long Term

A key part of Dr. Yáñez's approach at IHTC is preserving the donor area across your lifetime of potential hair loss. He never over-harvests in a single session to achieve a maximally ambitious first result — because doing so eliminates the option for a future second session. Safe extraction limits are always respected.

Hair Loss Progression Is Factored In

If you're under 35, or if your family history suggests continued aggressive hair loss, Dr. Yáñez builds your procedure plan around projected future loss — not just your current state. This includes placing the hairline at a position that will remain natural-looking even if surrounding native hair continues to thin, and reserving enough donor density for potential future work.

No Commitment at the Consultation

Whether you're planning a single session or thinking ahead to a two-session approach, the free consultation at IHTC gives you the full picture with no obligation. Dr. Yáñez will tell you what's achievable now, what might make sense later, and what the combined FUE+DHI procedure at IHTC would cost at each stage.

The bottom line on second sessions: having the option is better than not having it. That option is only available if your first surgeon planned conservatively enough. At IHTC, every procedure is planned with your long-term appearance in mind — not just the result on the day of surgery.

Frequently Asked Questions

The most common questions patients ask about graft count before booking their consultation at IHTC.

How many grafts do I need for a hairline?

A hairline restoration typically requires between 500 and 1,800 grafts, depending on the extent of recession and the density you want to achieve. An early Norwood II–III recession may need 500–1,000 grafts; a more significantly receded hairline at Stage III–IV may need 1,200–1,800 grafts. Dr. Erik Yáñez designs each hairline individually — your exact count is determined during a free consultation at IHTC.

How is graft count calculated?

Graft count is calculated by multiplying the surface area of your recipient zone (in cm²) by your target density (grafts per cm²). Typical transplant density ranges from 35 to 50 grafts per cm². Hair characteristics — thickness, curl, and color contrast with the scalp — also affect the final number. Dr. Yáñez additionally evaluates your donor area density to confirm the count is achievable without over-depleting the permanent zone.

Does more grafts mean better results?

Not necessarily. More grafts cover more area, but packing them too densely in a single session can reduce graft survival rates. The right graft count balances coverage and density with what your donor area can safely provide. At IHTC, Dr. Erik Yáñez uses the combined FUE+DHI procedure to maximize both placement precision and graft survival — the goal is the most natural result achievable, not the highest number possible.

What is the maximum number of grafts in one session?

Most surgeons can safely extract and implant between 2,000 and 4,000 grafts in a single session, depending on individual donor density and the size of the recipient zone. At IHTC, session limits are set based on your specific anatomy. For patients needing more than one session, Dr. Erik Yáñez plans both procedures at the initial consultation — preserving enough donor density to achieve the final result without over-harvesting in session one.
